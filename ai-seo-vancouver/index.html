"AI SEO" is really two things bundled together

When people search for "AI SEO," they're usually looking for one thing but actually need two: classic SEO, which gets a page ranked and crawled, and AI readiness, which determines whether an AI assistant that finds that page can actually understand it well enough to recommend it.

These aren't competing priorities — they're sequential. A page has to be found before it can be understood, so SEO fundamentals still come first. But once a page ranks reasonably well, AI readiness becomes the bigger unaddressed gap, because almost nobody has checked for it yet.

Questionsyou might be asking

Is "AI SEO" a real, distinct discipline?
It's more accurate to think of it as two things bundled under one popular search term: classic SEO (ranking) and AI readiness (being correctly understood once found). Both matter, and they require different checks.
Can a site have perfect SEO and still fail AI readiness?
Yes, and it's common. We have a real example: a Vancouver auto repair shop scored 100 on SEO and 87 on accessibility, and still passed 0 out of 2 AI readiness checks.
Which should I fix first, SEO or AI readiness?
If your site isn't ranking or getting crawled at all, fix SEO fundamentals first — none of the AI checks matter if nothing ever finds the page. If your site already ranks reasonably well, AI readiness is likely the bigger unaddressed gap.
