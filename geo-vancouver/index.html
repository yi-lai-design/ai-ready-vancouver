SEO, AEO, and GEO aren't the same thing

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of shaping content so an AI system is more likely to pull from it and cite it when generating a longer, synthesized answer — distinct from ranking (SEO) or directly answering one specific query (AEO).

The term originated in academic research studying how content characteristics affect visibility inside AI-generated answers, and has since become a broader shorthand for optimizing content for AI search generally. The underlying idea holds either way: a search engine ranks pages, but a generative engine synthesizes an answer out of several sources — and it has to choose which sources to actually use.

That's a different competition than ranking #1. A page can be the top Google result and still not get pulled into a generated answer if its content isn't structured in a way that's easy to extract and cite accurately.

Content that's easyto quote correctly

Research on generative engine optimization has consistently pointed toward the same few things: content that's clear, sourced, and unambiguous gets pulled into AI-generated answers more often than vague or purely persuasive copy.

A direct, unambiguous claim is easier for a system to extract and attribute correctly than something wrapped in marketing language.

Real numbers and data

Specific statistics are more citable — and more useful to a reader — than a general claim of being "the best."

Questionsyou might be asking

What's the difference between SEO, AEO, and GEO?
SEO is about ranking in search results. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) is about directly answering a specific question, the way a featured snippet or voice assistant does. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is about being the source an AI system pulls from and cites when it generates a longer, synthesized answer. They overlap but aren't the same thing.
Is GEO an academic term or a marketing term?
Both. It started as an academic research term studying how to make content more visible in AI-generated answers, and has since been adopted more loosely in marketing to describe the broader practice of optimizing for AI search.
Does GEO require different content than SEO?
Not entirely different, but different emphasis. SEO rewards keyword relevance and backlinks. GEO tends to reward content that's easy to extract and quote accurately: clear factual statements, real statistics, and unambiguous claims a generative system can lift out and attribute correctly.
